# RA2 near-miss analysis

We reviewed 77 historical matches and 14 recent port investigations. The case histories identify a community port as the source of 23 closures and local work as the source of 32. In 22 cases the available records do not establish who found the decisive change. Fifty cases have their own before/after source pair. For the remaining 27, the table identifies the available queue, ledger or sibling-source evidence.

The strongest recurring difference was how a source change was selected. Both teams varied local lifetimes, branches and helper boundaries. Community investigations often supplied a second caller, a sibling function or a version comparison that distinguished between plausible choices. In the bridge search, moving four later scan cursors into their branches and separating the two wood-bridge paths resolved the earlier stack-slot mismatch. In the statistics serializer, applying the donor's field overload wholesale made the native result worse. The native version still differs by one byte in 5,056.

Of the 14 recent functions, nine have zero differences after masking relocations. Six also have complete binding verification. The bridge is among the other three, with 11 of 72 bindings resolved. Five functions remain partial. These results come from the recorded builds and their controls.
