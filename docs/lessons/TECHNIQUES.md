# How community sources closed RA2 near misses

**The history supports changing how we choose the next source form.** Of 77 cases, the evidence attributes 23 closures to a community port and 32 to our work. For 22, the original finder is unestablished. Call form, helper or inline boundaries account for ten cases on each side. Type, scope or temporary materialization accounts for five community and eight local closures. The record gives no basis for saying our team never tries either technique.

The clearest difference is in finding a candidate worth testing. Four community-attributed cases have a sibling or whole-body transfer as their primary technique. Our stronger local examples already inspect callees and lifetimes, but the logs rarely identify a specific external witness before a search. Across the 127 ledger rows associated with these 77 addresses, the “last forms tried” field mentions a sibling or look-alike in one row, a sweep/grid/variant in 16 rows, and neither similar.py nor callees.py by name. These are literal, case-insensitive text counts on abbreviated records. They measure what was documented; source comparisons and the work queue establish what was actually done.

**First change to try.** Before reopening a parked function, identify one matched operation in a sibling or donor, state which native instruction it predicts and archive a whole-donor baseline. Test genuine lifetimes, call boundaries and control flow on that baseline. The three-byte bridge case closed on an adapted donor; the one-byte statistics case survived the same treatment. Escalation should depend on new evidence and a testable prediction.

The recent comparison and the 77-case history overlap. Their counts answer different questions and must be read separately. The target list also includes explicitly labelled binding or integration follow-ups where the source already matches.

## What the recent YR ports changed in RA2

Nine of the fourteen reviewed RA2 functions reached zero differences against the 1.001 target after relocation bytes were masked. Six also have complete relocation verification. `One_Time`, `Draw_Pixel_Effects` and `Can_Repair_Bridge` reached zero masked differences with binding checks still open. ## What changes the search

1. **Find a witness before generating more variants.** At the first persistent mismatch, record its instruction, operand, target, or branch destination. Search matched functions for the *same operation*, ranking candidates by direct callees, argument order, instruction fragments and shared types. Examine the leading candidate and one plausible counterexample. For example, the matched division operator in the quaternion TU was a poor template when expanded at its caller. The indexed access in Build_Matrix3D and Normalize was the useful witness. W1 and W3 already cover whole-body ports and cross-version comparison. The missing step is to record which operation transferred and why the target should emit it.
2. **Recover where a computation lives.** For each helper visible in the reference, record whether it emits a call, expands inline, or leaves no instructions. Compare a sibling using the same header. Then try the helper at the same nesting level, with its real parameter, result, scope and type. A named value must have a consumer. Splitting a Boolean expression, moving a constructor into a helper, or replacing a scalar pair with an aggregate can change several register choices at once. Our C1/C2/C4/C7/C15/C16 cover these mechanisms individually. The recent cases call for checking them *before* opening a grid of interchangeable spellings.
3. **Read the old attempt bank after an independent reconstruction when it could anchor the search.** On 20 November the YR team described AI_Build_Defense stalling through nine attempts. A fresh investigator used the executable, callee list, similar functions and TibSun first. Three useful register-eligible locals ultimately closed it. YR's review of 47 previously stalled functions also singles out useful locals and shared helpers. Those are their 47 cases. The historical population below is ours. A fresh pass is an experiment: batch 54's genuinely blind Make_Closest draft still missed 337 positions in the target interval and emitted 64 extra bytes.
4. **Distinguish a local minimum from a version boundary.** Test the whole donor body, the closest RA2 sibling and a native reconstruction as separate baselines. Record the first divergence, changed call graph, and smallest winning combination against the donor baseline. Batch 54's bridge required three edits together on the donor form. Statistics retained one byte after 1,380 historical sources were recompiled and 23 new distinct forms measured. For that site the earlier stop decision still stands. A new donor or a newly identified instruction witness is a reason to reopen a case. An unchanged score alone supplies no reason.
5. **Keep the stop rule and the release gate separate.** YR's PLAN cites 30 checks or 60 minutes and a 15-minute permutation run above 90% when describing Byte Tactics. Its own permuter section describes a late, stalled 95%-plus search and roughly 260,000 unsuccessful compiles on three established near misses. The single-function November rounds emphasize callee/sibling evidence, banked drafts and cross-executable checks. Our W5/D1/D5 already stop expensive repeated searches. Extend each parked record with the evidence needed to reopen it and a prediction that would disprove the proposed explanation.
6. **Make lessons searchable by their evidence.** After a batch, add the working source form, reference sibling and RVA, affected calls or operands, result in each game version and regression scope to the guide. Gibbo's guide-keeper and merge gate serve different jobs: one records how a result was discovered, the other reruns existing matches. Our REMATCH audit also records naturalness and claim identity. Keep the two checks even when a target has zero masked differences. The bridge still has 61 unverified bindings.
