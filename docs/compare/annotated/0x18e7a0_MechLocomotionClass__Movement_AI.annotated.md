## Why we did not find it

**Partly.** The ledger and batch 16 show a drop from 3,595 to 1,891 differences. Batch 17 reached 1,081 by *extending* head-coordinate and old-cell lifetimes, using a tube-height helper and keeping distinct dispatch returns. Batch 18 reached 1,070. Batch 22 already had the right frame and 63 calls. Its overlay grid reached 82 disassembly hunks but 1,781 positional differences, so the 1,070-byte version remained. Those numbers describe their own TU snapshots. They explain why a greedy pass would preserve the opposite lifetime choice. This replay gives a direct example in H9, where an independently flat edit becomes necessary in the winning context.

## How they found it

Gibbo's November 23 Mech entry in docs/rounds/2026-11.md describes reading the repeated stores in the executable as VC6 tail duplication, naming the changed-cell condition for two tests, putting the failed-zone guard first and consulting the already matched Hover sibling for the tube exit. Their earlier attempt rows record 89.1% for the sibling-else/bridge form, 89.5% after repeating the raw path access, and 54 return-form combinations without an improvement. Diff 2 shows which Hover-inspired tube details stayed different in RA2. The measured neutral tunnel changes above give no support for crediting the RA2 match to those differences alone.
